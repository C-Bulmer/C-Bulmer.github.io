Kingdom Hearts: Birth By Sleep is the 6th game in the Kingdom Hearts series. After I beat the first Kingdom Hearts game I was told to play Birth By Sleep next, because despite being the 6th game release it is the first game chronologically (At least in the Kingdom Hearts -HD 1.5+2.5 ReMIX), I have also been told it only gets more confusing from here. The game follows 3 keyblade wielders, Terra, Aqua, and Ventus on their journey to stop creatures made of repressed emotions called Unversed. The game delves into the beginnings of Xehanort's plan to open the door to Kingdom Hearts and his search for the seven princesses of heart. The game was originally released on the PSP, and while the graphics were updated for the remaster, there's still hints of its portable origins in its gameplay. The game is divided into three separate campaigns, one for each of the characters, that take roughly 6-8 hours each (I completed Aqua's campaign first in 8 hours, and Terra's last in 6, whether this is a result of a shorter campaign or I simply got better after running through 3 times I can't quite tell). These campaigns can be further divided into worlds which take anywhere from 10 minutes to an hour to complete. The game has segmented perfectly so that no matter if you're playing in a doctor's lobby or a long plane ride, there's enough content to simply pull out the PSP and play. (I've always been fascinated by the gameplay design implemented by the era of 2010s handheld devices. As opposed to conventional consoles these devices could be pulled out and played pretty much anywhere, so gameplay had to be as easy to pick up and play as the handhelds themselves. I'm quite sad that true handheld devices have died out D:). The PSP elements are also reflected in one of the worlds present in the game, the Mirage Arena. Players would go here to engage in local multiplayer activities such as board games, racing, and fighting enemies together. While Kingdom Hearts -HD 1.5+2.5 ReMIX has deprecated the local multiplayer you can still do these activities, albeit with COM opponents instead. The combat is pretty similar to standard Kingdom Hearts combat with one exception, the command deck. Abilities such as magic or special attacks are items called commands. Each command has its own level, and when it reaches max level you can merge it with another command to create a stronger command, furthermore you can also use a crystal to add abilities to these commands which become unlocked permanently when the command reaches max level. This results in an ever changing play style, incentivizing players to swap out the commands to make better ones and get more abilities. And towards the end of the game you have several strong abilities that can defeat your enemies with ease, or equip the same one several times if you want (In order to unlock the secret ending on the standard difficulty I was playing, it requires you to defeat a total of 9,999 Unversed. A task I completed by equipping the strongest spell in the game, Mega Flare, 4 times and going into a room filled with Unversed. Sorry Vanitas!). Kingdom Hearts as a series is a weird patchwork of vastly different styles of games (There's a JRPG, a card game, a rhythm game, and even a Pokemon clone), but that's what gives it it's heart. Just like how the story is a mix of several Disney properties and Final Fantasy, the game has this charm that could only come from the absurdity and creativity that was so very prominent in the early 2000s, and I am excited to see what else this series has in store for me.
